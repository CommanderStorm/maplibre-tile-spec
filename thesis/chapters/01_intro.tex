\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch_introduction}

Interactive web maps have become a ubiquitous interface for geographic information, powering applications from navigation and logistics to urban planning and journalism.
Modern map renderers such as MapLibre GL~JS and Mapbox GL~JS deliver rich, client-side rendered vector maps by streaming tiled geometry data to the browser, where a client side style specification drives a WebGL- or WebGPU-based rendering pipeline.
This architecture enables smooth panning, zooming, and rotation, but it also places competing demands on the network (tile fetching), the CPU (decoding, style expression evaluation), and the GPU (draw calls)- all within a single frame budget.

As map deployments scale - from personal projects to platforms serving millions of daily users - the cost of suboptimal performance compounds.
Larger tiles increase transfer time and bandwidth cost; redundant style layers waste rendering cycles; unnecessary properties inflate decoding overhead.
On mobile devices, these inefficiencies translate directly into battery drain and thermal throttling, though direct energy measurement is outside the scope of this work.
On unstable network connections, such as on trains or aeroplanes, these translate to degraded user experiences.

Despite this, the map rendering and geospatial community has largely treated style authoring and data preparation as independent concerns: styles are hand-tuned for visual appearance, while tiles are encoded with general-purpose content and compression.
The interaction between the two is left unexploited.

\section{Research Goals}\label{sec_research_goals}

This thesis investigates automatic, style- and data-driven co-optimisation for MapLibre-based map rendering pipelines.
Three research questions guide the work:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*}]
    \item To what extent \textbf{can automatic data and style co-optimisation improve rendering performance and reduce map tile size}?
    \item \textbf{Which techniques} - such as expression simplification, dead layer elimination, layer merging, tile shaving, and columnar re-encoding - \textbf{are most effective}, \textbf{and how do they interact}?
    \item \textbf{How expensive are these optimisations} in terms of preprocessing time, and what are the \textbf{tradeoffs between decoding latency, rendering throughput, and transfer size}?
\end{enumerate}

To answer these questions, the thesis designs and implements a standalone optimiser that consumes a MapLibre style JSON document and, optionally, tile-level statistics, and produces a semantically equivalent style whose rendering performance is measurably improved - together with optimised tile data.

All transformations are visually lossless:
The optimised output must produce pixel-identical rendering for every tile at every zoom level.

\section{Contributions}\label{sec_contributions}

The main contributions of this thesis are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A \textbf{three-level optimisation pipeline} comprising expression-level rewrites (constant folding, algebraic simplification, stats-driven folding, selectivity reordering), structural passes (dead layer elimination, metadata refinement, layer merging), and data-level transformations (tile shaving, encoding strategy selection), modeled on peephole optimisers and query planners from the compiler and database literature.

    \item \textbf{Style-driven tile shaving} that computes a pruning advisory from the optimised style, enabling removal of unused properties, geometry types, property values, and feature IDs from tile data.  Tile shaving is the dominant contributor to load-time improvements; style-level passes assist by narrowing the advisory, though the interaction is additive rather than synergistic (\cref{sub_mlt_discussion}).

    \item An \textbf{automatic encoding strategy selection} for the MapLibre Tiles (MLT) columnar format that reformulates tile encoding as a multi-strategy competition over sort orderings, integer encodings, and string compression strategies, adapting to the changed data characteristics that emerge after style-driven shaving alters value distributions and column cardinalities.

    \item An \textbf{empirical evaluation} across 15 styles, 18 scenarios, and 17 cumulative ablation steps, validated end-to-end on two styles with tile data, demonstrating measurable improvements in tile volume, load time, and rendering stability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Outline}\label{sec_outline}

The remainder of this thesis is structured as follows.
\cref{ch_related_work} surveys tile data pruning, encoding formats, style-level tooling, and rendering benchmarking, identifying the gap that motivates a joint style-data optimiser.
\cref{ch_theoretical_background} establishes the compiler, database, and encoding foundations that the optimiser draws on, and formalises the visual-equivalence-constrained optimisation problem.
\cref{ch_methods} presents the two-phase pipeline - expression-level rewrites and structural passes - together with style-driven tile shaving, automatic encoding strategy selection, and the cumulative ablation methodology.
\cref{ch_experiments} evaluates each optimisation level in isolation and combined, tests generalisation across 15 styles, and quantifies the full pipeline's end-to-end effect on tile volume, load time, and rendering stability.
\cref{ch_conclusion} revisits the research questions, summarises the key findings, and identifies directions for future work.
